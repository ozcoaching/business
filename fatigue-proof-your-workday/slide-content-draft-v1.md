## 4. Fatigue 101 (3 min)
- **Physical fatigue:** muscles tire, force drops, strain rises
- **Mental fatigue:** attention drops, errors and shortcuts rise
- They feed each other
- Fatigue reduces comfort, raises injury risk, lowers performance
*Notes:* Fatigue is not weakness. It is a normal signal. The goal is to catch it early.
*Offshore lens:* Tired body + tired mind = higher risk. Know both.

## 5. Area 1: Recognise it early (3 min)
**Body signs:** stiffness, soreness, tingling or numbness, weaker grip, clumsiness
**Mind signs:** rereading, missed details, irritability, "autopilot", shortcuts
*Notes:* Fatigue builds quietly. It rarely gives dramatic warnings (source: micro-break meeting kit, Safety Now). Early signs are the cheapest time to act.
*Offshore lens:* Spot it in yourself and your crew. Speak up early.

## 7. Area 2: Spot the hidden demands (3 min)
- Repetition
- Static or awkward positions
- Workload and time pressure
- Few real breaks
*Notes:* These add up across a week, even with a good setup. A perfect chair does not remove repetition.

## 8. Same fatigue, different workplaces (4 min)
| Office | Offshore |
|---|---|
| Prolonged sitting | Pipe handling, heavy lifting and positioning |
| Repetitive admin and screen work | Inspecting equipment and tools |
| Few scheduled breaks | Stairs and ladders |
| Mental clarity and stress | Confined spaces, awkward postures |
*Notes:* Awkward postures in confined spaces add physical strain and mental load (focus on the task and the environment). Documentation work offshore is the "office" part of the job.
*Offshore lens:* Know your high-demand tasks. Plan breaks around them.

## 10. Area 3: Work with your energy (3 min)
- Put demanding tasks in your best-focus hours, where you can
- Batch similar tasks; reduce switching
- Protect focus: one task at a time
- Say what you need: pace, help, rotation of tasks
*Notes:* Not everyone controls their schedule. Focus on what your role allows. For offshore, this may be sequencing within a task or crew rotation of heavy tasks.

## 11. The industrial athlete (3 min)
**Your job is physical performance. Prepare like it.**
- Warm up before demanding tasks
- Build and keep strength and fitness
- Manage load across the shift and the week
- Recover on purpose
*Notes:* Borrowed from sports medicine: preparation, load management and recovery. Trade sources describe it as a holistic approach (OH&S Online, 2023). [VERIFY / ADD Dr. Wahl's specific concepts: see open items.]
*Offshore lens:* Treat the shift like a game. Prepare, perform, recover.

## 12. Area 4: Make recovery count (3 min)
- Micro-breaks: 30-60 seconds every 20-30 minutes (meeting-kit guidance)
- Rest or stretch both work (Univ. of Alberta, 2024)
- Meta-analysis: micro-breaks reduce fatigue and boost vigour; effects are small but real (Albulescu 2022)
- Heavy mental load? Longer breaks help more
*Notes:* Be honest: micro-breaks help, they are not a cure. Effects are strongest when they are regular.

## 13. Between shifts: the recovery basics (4 min)
- **Sleep:** protect it; dark, cool, quiet; consistent routine
- **Hydration:** drink steadily through the shift
- **Fuel:** regular meals; protein and carbs for energy; limit heavy meals and stimulants late in the day
- **Move daily:** walk, mobility, strength
*Notes:* Individual-control items only. Offshore rosters often use 12-hour shifts; sleep can be short after rotation changes. Do not go beyond: this is not medical advice. [VERIFY specific sleep and hydration numbers before adding any.]
*Offshore lens:* Use your off-hours to recover. Skip what steals sleep.
